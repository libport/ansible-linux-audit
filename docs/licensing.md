## What MIT permits

The copyright holder retains copyright while granting broad rights to use,
copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, sublicense, and sell the software.
Redistributions must preserve the copyright and permission notice. MIT places
no noncommercial or field-of-use restriction on recipients.

The license applies only to material whose rights are held by the project owner
or that the owner is otherwise authorized to license. A repository license
cannot waive another party's copyright, trademark, patent, contract, privacy,
or publicity rights.

## Why third-party benchmarks are excluded

Downloading or purchasing access to a benchmark does not transfer its
copyright. In particular, the public terms for non-member CIS products include
restrictions concerning public posting, direct derivative works, and claims of
compliance. A noncommercial restriction is also incompatible with the usual
Open Source Definition because open-source recipients must be allowed to use
software commercially.

Consequently this repository must not contain a CIS-authored or CIS-derived
catalog, including copied or closely paraphrased:

- recommendation titles and explanatory text;
- control identifiers and curated ordering;
- profile assignments or mappings;
- audit and remediation procedures; or
- machine-readable benchmark exports.

Relevant policies can be reviewed at:

- <https://www.cisecurity.org/terms-of-use-for-non-member-cis-products>
- <https://www.cisecurity.org/cis-logos-and-trademark-use-policy>
- <https://opensource.org/osd>

DFSA controls instead use original identifiers, wording, organization, and
implementations. Citations identify technical sources; they do not import the
source's expressive content or license.

## Dependencies and extensions

No third-party source code, benchmark catalog, or documentation is vendored in
this repository. External technical documentation is cited only as reference
material. Ansible, Python, and development packages are separately installed
dependencies that retain their own licenses and are not relicensed as MIT
here.

Private external catalogs and task files are supplied by the operator and do
not become MIT-licensed merely because DFSA loads them. Operators are
responsible for their authorization, licensing, and behavior.

## Project identity

The repository slug and software name use neutral, independently authored
project branding. The project uses no third-party benchmark logo, badge,
certification mark, or compliance claim. Technical and licensing references do
not imply affiliation, approval, or certification.

Names and visual branding should be reviewed again before each promoted
release. Obtain qualified legal review before adopting any third-party mark or
making a compatibility or compliance claim.

This document records the project's licensing policy; it is not legal advice
for a particular jurisdiction.
